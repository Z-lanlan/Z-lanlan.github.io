From Messy Data to a Dashboard

Cleaning, Summarizing, and Visualizing a Fictional Season of Game Streaming

Lanlan ZhuDepartment of Information Technology and Decision SciencesG. Brint Ryan College of BusinessUniversity of North TexasEmail: lanlan.zhu@unt.edu

Learning Objectives

In this lesson, we will follow a fictional game-streaming dataset from raw data to an interactive dashboard. We will generate the data using AI, clean and integrate it in Excel, explore patterns with summary statistics, and visualize the findings in Tableau.

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Generate: Create and verify a realistic fictional dataset using an AI prompt.
  • Clean: Identify and fix data-quality issues, combine related tables, and prepare an Excel Table using TRIM, PROPER, CONCAT, VALUE, and XLOOKUP.
  • Analyze: Summarize data with PivotTables and SUMIFS, and interpret basic statistics such as mean, median, standard deviation, and correlation.
  • Visualize: Build clear charts in Tableau, including bar, line, and scatter plots, explore a Sankey diagram, and combine visualizations into a dashboard to communicate findings.

Lesson Overview

Using a fictional fall 2026 game-streaming dataset, we will examine 600 streaming sessions, a Games table, and a daily Weather table. Starting with a prediction about which game genre has the most total watch hours, we will generate and verify the data, clean and analyze it in Excel, and build a Tableau dashboard to explore patterns and communicate insights.

Topics Covered

  • Data Generation: AI prompting with ChatGPT or Claude and verification of fictional data
  • Data Cleaning and Integration in Excel: TRIM, PROPER, CONCAT, VALUE, XLOOKUP, and Excel Tables
  • Data Summarization and Statistical Analysis: PivotTables, SUMIFS, AVERAGE, MEDIAN, STDEV.S, and CORREL
  • Data Visualization in Tableau: Bar, line, and scatter charts, a Sankey extension, and an interactive dashboard

Assignment 1

Statistics: Weather and Streaming Viewers

Join the daily Weather data to streaming sessions by date using XLOOKUP. Use COUNTIFS and AVERAGEIFS to compare viewing across weather conditions, excluding two viral streams (AvgViewers ≥ 15,000) from the weather averages. Explore the relationship between precipitation and viewership with CORREL.

Excel formulas

Use the weather condition in cell A25 for the first two formulas.

COUNTIFS (by weather)=COUNTIFS(Streams[Condition],A25,Streams[AvgViewers],"<15000")
AVERAGEIFS (by weather)=AVERAGEIFS(Streams[AvgViewers],Streams[Condition],A25,Streams[AvgViewers],"<15000")
AVERAGEIFS (wet days)=AVERAGEIFS(Streams[AvgViewers],Streams[PrecipIn],">0",Streams[AvgViewers],"<15000")
AVERAGEIFS (dry days)=AVERAGEIFS(Streams[AvgViewers],Streams[PrecipIn],0,Streams[AvgViewers],"<15000")
CORREL=CORREL(Streams[AvgViewers],Streams[PrecipIn])

Weather check: Wet days draw 29% more viewers

Average viewers per stream by weather condition (2 viral streams excluded).

1,369Wet days (rain or storm)
1,062Dry days (sunny or cloudy)
Interpret with care. CORREL(PrecipIn, AvgViewers) is only 0.03. In this example, the wet-versus-dry distinction is more informative than the amount of precipitation. Storm has only 19 streams, and correlation does not establish causation.
